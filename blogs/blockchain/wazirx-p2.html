Decoding WazirX Assets Breach (Part 2)

Following the attacker on-chain: how the multisig’s upgrade mechanism was abused and where the stolen funds went.

The main cause of WazirX asset breach is due to the flaw in the multisig wallet’s contract upgrade mechanism.

If you didn’t read the first part, click here to read it.

The hacker has multiple accounts while performing this attack. Few accounts linked in the attack are:

  1. WazirX Exploiter (Primary Account) (Address: 0x04b21735E93Fa3f8df70e2Da89e6922616891a88)
  2. WazirX Exploiter 5 (Address: 0x6EeDF92Fb92Dd68a270c3205e96DCCc527728066)

WazirX Exploiter 5 was funded 0.1 ETH 6 times by Tornado Cash on July 10, 2024 between 6:28 AM UTC and 6:41 AM UTC.

So, what is Tornado Cash and why did the hacker use it?

Tornado Cash: Tornado Cash is a crypto mixer and is used to maintain anonymity in the public blockchain. Initially, users deposit an amount to the Tornado Cash contract. Amount to be deposited is fixed amount like 0.1 ETH, 1 ETH, 10 ETH and 100 ETH. After depositing, they will get a private key note which can be used while withdrawing money. And to withdraw money, you can use different account along with the private key note to maintain anonymity. So, if you deposit money using account A and withdraw using account B, no one will know that account A also belongs to you since there will be multiple transactions with same amount value in that contract.

So, hacker might have used another account to deposit 0.1 ETH into Tornado Cash long ago and withdrew the ETH using WazirX Exploiter 5 on July 10, 2024. This shows that the attack was well planned and prepared long in advance.

On the same day, July 10th at 7:37 AM UTC, WazirX Exploiter 5 deployed a malicious smart contract (0xEf279….35fC6) to launch the phishing attack and steal the private keys.

And at 7:49 AM UTC, he performed test transactions using SHIB in WazirX Exploiter 5 from a multisig 0x09B33E75E51Ae9A30EcA4B65Db76E511199af664.

@zachxbt tweeted in X that he was able to demix and find 6 × 0.1 ETH matching deposits made the day before from 0xc68. He tweeted that demix is accurate as 0xc68 was also doing tests with 0x304 multisig on July 9th with SHIB.

Anyhow, they required the private keys of three WazirX signatories and one Liminal signatory to perform exploitation.

The signature exploitation sequence

Liminal explained on their blog on WazirX incident about the signature exploitation sequences:

  1. When Liminal received first GALA transaction with the victim 1 (Here, victim refers to WazirX signatories), Liminal rejected the transaction due to signature mismatch. However, the moment the user sent the signed transaction to Liminal for approval, the signature was already exposed to the attackers owing to the victim’s compromised machine. It is evident that the attacker compromised the machine, and they were able to change the payload using a sophisticated MITM attack or similar client side compromise. A forensic analysis of the victim’s signer machines will likely uncover more details and help address the root cause of this issue.
  2. Since victim 1 wasn’t able to create GALA transaction, victim 2 created the GALA transaction and Liminal rejected it once again due to tx data mismatch. Assumption is that, the attacker has compromised the machine, which explains the mismatch in payload emerging from the transactions generated by victim 2 as well. This is likely to have been executed using a sophisticated MITM attack or similar client side compromise.

    Still the method hacker used to obtain first two signatures is unknown.
  3. Then, victim 1 created a legitimate USDT transaction with 2 valid signatures. This transaction was crafted to look legitimate but was designed to fail. Main motive of collecting first two signatures is to upgrade the multisig to a malicious contract rather than for the USDT transfer itself. When victim 3 tried to approve the transaction, his machine was also compromised due to the injected payload by the hacker and got the third signature he wanted.
    (Failed TX hash: 0x8b99ae63…0588ef)
  4. The final malicious transaction was crafted in such a way that the fields used to verify policies were using legit transaction details and had valid signatures. So, Liminal signed the transaction which resulted in compromise of all four required signatures of the multisig wallet.

The delegatecall upgrade

Then, to facilitate the attack, hacker executed a delegate call from the multisig wallet, pointing to their malicious contract.

delegatecall(): delegatecall allows a contract to execute code from another contract while keeping the calling contract’s storage, msg.sender, and msg.value unchanged. The code of the target contract runs in the context of the calling contract, meaning any state changes affect the calling contract.

This call altered the slot0 of the multisig proxy to the phishing contract address. The delegate call data is:

0x804e1f0a000000000000000000000000ef279c2ab14960aa319008cbea384b9f8ac35fc6

The first 4 bytes, 0x804e1f0a, correspond to the function selector of the target contract’s (multisig wallet) function that the delegate call is invoking.

The remaining bytes, 000000000000000000000000ef279c2ab14960aa319008cbea384b9f8ac35fc6, represent the parameter(s) passed to the function. In this case, the parameter is an address 0xef279c2ab14960aa319008cbea384b9f8ac35fc6 (Phishing contract).

So, whenever a phishing contract calls a function in a multisig wallet, the multisig wallet will run the function on the phishing contract’s address (since msg.sender = 0xef2….5fc6).

From this point forward, every transaction to the Safe Multisig redirected a delegate call to the new malicious phishing contract address. This allowed the attackers to continuously transfer funds from the WazirX multisig wallet to their WazirX Exploiter Account.

Total assets stolen by the hackers

After stealing, hacker converted $150 million of ERC-20 tokens to Ether to avoid getting funds frozen.

Crypto Freezing: Cryptocurrency freezes are funds that are currently unavailable or temporarily blocked. If exchanges or financial platforms suspect fraud or money laundering, they can freeze the funds in the account.

Spotonchain tweeted in X that,

“The total ETH holding of the WazirX attacker has risen to 59,097 ETH ($201M)!

  1. 15,298 ETH ($52M) was stolen directly from the exchange;
  2. 43,799 ETH ($149M) was acquired by dumping various assets, notably including:
    • 5.43T SHIB ($101M at the time of the hack) has been sold for 26,535 ETH at an average price of $0.00001693 ($92M);
    • 20.5M MATIC ($11.3M at the time of the hack) has been sold for 2,968 ETH at an average price of $0.499 ($10.2M);
    • 640.27B PEPE ($7.58M at the time of the hack) has been sold for 2,170 ETH at an average price of $0.00001168 ($7.48M).”
